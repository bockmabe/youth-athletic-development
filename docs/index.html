Youth athletic development

One short session, twice a week

Acceleration, coordination, single-leg stability and controlled stopping for a growing young athlete in sports like jiu-jitsu and ultimate. Do it twice a week on non-consecutive days.

1 Warm-up

5 min

A-march2 × 10 m

Watch on YouTubeBrendan Thompson — Speed & Physical Therapy · 3:22

Dose
2 sets of 10 m. Walk back between sets.

Cues

  • Stand tall, like a string pulls the top of your head up.
  • Toe up, knee up: lift the knee to about hip height with the toes pulled up.
  • Step down under your hips, not out in front.
  • Opposite arm and leg; elbows bent, hands swing cheek to hip.

Common errors

  • Leaning back to get the knee higher.
  • Toes pointing down at the top.
  • Rushing; this is a slow, crisp rhythm drill.
A-skip2 × 10 m

Watch on YouTubeBrendan Thompson — Speed & Physical Therapy · 2:18 (A-skip first, then A-run)

Dose
2 sets of 10 m. Walk back between sets.

Cues

  • Same shape as the A-march, now with a skip rhythm.
  • Punch the foot down under the hip; let the ground pop you up.
  • Quick, quiet contacts on the ball of the foot.
  • Stay tall; the arms keep the rhythm.

Common errors

  • Reaching the foot out in front and landing on the heel.
  • Loud, flat-footed contacts.
  • Arms swinging across the body.

2 Strength & stability

10 min

Split squat2 × 8 / leg

Watch on YouTubeE3 Rehab Exercise Library · 0:13 demo

Dose
2 sets of 8 reps on each leg. Bodyweight.

Cues

  • Feet hip-width apart, like standing on two train tracks, not a tightrope.
  • Lower straight down until the back knee is just above the floor.
  • Whole front foot stays flat; push through it to stand.
  • Front knee tracks over the middle toes. A hand on a wall is fine.

Common errors

  • Narrow stance that makes you wobble.
  • Front heel lifting off the floor.
  • Front knee caving inward.
  • Dropping fast and bouncing out of the bottom.
Single-leg Romanian deadlift2 × 6 / leg

Watch on YouTubeE3 Rehab Exercise Library · 0:20 demo

Dose
2 sets of 6 reps on each leg. Bodyweight.

Cues

  • Soft bend in the standing knee.
  • Hinge at the hip: chest goes forward as the free leg reaches straight back.
  • Hips square to the floor; back toes point down.
  • Only go as low as you can keep your balance and a flat back. Fingertips on a wall are fine.

Common errors

  • Hip of the free leg opening up toward the ceiling.
  • Rounding the back to reach lower.
  • Squatting down instead of hinging.
Calf raises2 × 12

Watch on YouTubeE3 Rehab Exercise Library · 0:20 demo

Dose
2 sets of 12 reps, both feet. Light hand support for balance.

Cues

  • Rise as high as you can, pressing through the big toe.
  • Pause for a moment at the top.
  • Lower slowly, about two to three seconds.
  • Knees straight but not locked.

Common errors

  • Bouncing through short, fast reps.
  • Rolling out onto the little toes.
  • Leaning on the support instead of just balancing.
Side plank2 × 20 s / side

Watch on YouTubeChildren's Hospital Colorado · 1:10

Dose
2 holds of 20 seconds on each side.

Cues

  • Elbow directly under the shoulder.
  • Straight line from head through hips to feet.
  • Push the floor away so the shoulder doesn't sink.
  • Keep breathing. The bent-knee version is fine if form slips.

Common errors

  • Hips sagging toward the floor.
  • Rolling the chest forward or back.
  • Holding your breath.

3 Coordination & acceleration

5–10 min

Small hop and stick2 × 5 / side

Watch on YouTubeStreamline Performance Physical Therapy · 0:20 demo

Dose
2 sets of 5 hops on each leg. Small hops.

Cues

  • Small hop, landing on the same leg.
  • Land quietly: ball of the foot, then heel; bend at hip and knee.
  • Knee lines up over the middle toes.
  • Freeze for 2–3 seconds before stepping down.

Common errors

  • Knee caving inward on landing.
  • Landing stiff-legged and loud.
  • Wobbling into extra hops to catch your balance. Make the hop smaller.
10-meter accelerations4 reps · 60–80%

Watch on YouTubeALTIS · 0:31 demo (2- and 3-point starts)

Dose
4 reps of 10 m at 60–80% effort to start. Walk back to recover fully.

Cues

  • Start from a staggered stance, body leaning forward.
  • Push the ground back behind you; rise gradually.
  • Strong, relaxed arm swing.
  • Build effort across the reps, from about 60% to 80%.

Common errors

  • Popping straight upright on the first step.
  • Reaching the foot far out in front.
  • Going all-out from rep one.
  • Cutting the walk-back rest short.
Run and controlled stop3 × 10 m

Watch on YouTubeE3 Rehab Exercise Library · 0:17 demo

Dose
3 reps: run 10 m, then stop under control. Use a speed you can stop from cleanly.

Cues

  • Shorten and quicken your steps as you slow down.
  • Sink your hips; chest stays over your knees.
  • Finish in a balanced athletic stance, feet about shoulder-width.
  • Hold the stop still for two seconds.

Common errors

  • Slamming on one straight, stiff leg.
  • Leaning way back to brake.
  • Knees collapsing inward.
  • Running faster than you can stop from cleanly.

Train smart

  • Twice a week, non-consecutive days (for example Monday and Thursday). Leave at least one day between sessions.
  • Progress on movement quality, not on effort or load. Clean, controlled reps come first. Keep the doses as written.
  • Stop the drill for the day after repeated stumbling or uncontrolled landings. That is a signal, not a reason to push harder.
  • Stop for pain. Don't train through pain, or through anything that changes how you move.
  • Heel pain, pain just below the kneecap, or other aches that keep returning after activity can be overuse at growth areas, which is common in active, growing kids. Rest from what hurts and have a pediatrician or sports-medicine clinician take a look.
  • Coordination changes that persist, get worse, or affect only one side deserve a professional assessment.

Some teens notice a temporary dip in coordination during fast growth; many don't. Research suggests that at least some adolescents show short-term delays in some balance and motor measures, and that the evidence is still incomplete. A growth spurt is not a reason to expect clumsiness, and this guide is not a diagnosis of anything.

This is general education, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or any concern, check with your pediatrician first. An adult watching for technique is a good idea, as the AAP report recommends supervision and proper technique.
